# Research Desk Doctrine

The operating rules for finding products to run in Q4 2026 and beyond.
Every session on this desk follows this document. When it and a gut feeling disagree, the doctrine wins until we change the doctrine on purpose.

> Status: **v0.1 draft.** Built before the reference screenshot was available. Sections marked `[MIRROR]` get rewritten to match it once it's shared.

---

## 1. The ten rules

1. **Signal before story.** A product enters the pipeline because of evidence (sales velocity, ad spend, search growth, comment demand), not because it's cool.
2. **Demand is proven, never assumed.** If people aren't already paying for it, or for something close to it, it's a research project, not a product.
3. **Every claim gets a source and a date.** "It's blowing up" is not evidence. "14 new Meta ads in 7 days, oldest still running at 41 days" is.
4. **Kill fast, kill cheap.** Most candidates die at the scorecard. That's the system working. A dead candidate costs ten minutes. A bad launch costs the month.
5. **Red-team everything.** Before any verdict the Skeptic argues the case *against*. If nobody can answer the Skeptic, it doesn't run.
6. **Margin is a filter, not an afterthought.** Below roughly a 3x landed-cost markup, Q4 CPMs eat the profit. Do the math before falling in love.
7. **The calendar is the boss.** In Q4 a great product that arrives late is a bad product. Fulfillment time decides what we can sell, and when.
8. **Angles beat products.** The same item can flop or print depending on who it's for and how it's framed. Every candidate needs at least three distinct angles before testing.
9. **Saturation is about the angle, not the product.** A product everyone sells can still work with a fresh angle, audience or offer. A product nobody sells may have no market.
10. **Write it down.** Every signal, score and verdict goes in [`LEDGER.md`](./LEDGER.md). The desk remembers so we don't re-research the same thing twice.

## 2. Desk roles

One person (you) plus Claude, wearing different hats. Each analysis names which hat is speaking.

| Role | Job | Asks |
|---|---|---|
| **Scout** | Finds raw signals | What's moving? Where did I see it? How fast? |
| **Analyst** | Turns signals into evidence | Is the demand real, growing, and buyable? |
| **Merchant** | Unit economics and logistics | Can we land it, ship it in time, and keep money after ads? |
| **Creative Director** | Angles, hooks, offers | Who's it for, what's the scroll-stopping moment, why now? |
| **Skeptic** | Red team | Why will this fail? Who already owns it? What's the hidden risk? |

## 3. The pipeline

Every candidate moves left to right. It can be killed at any stage.

```
SIGNAL  →  CANDIDATE  →  SCORED  →  RED-TEAMED  →  VERDICT
(raw)      (framed)      (100-pt)   (Skeptic)      RUN / TEST / WATCH / KILL
```

**Stage 1: Signal intake.** Log what was seen, where, when, and the raw number (views, orders, ad count, price). No judgment yet.

**Stage 2: Candidate framing.** Turn the signal into a sellable hypothesis in one line:
> *"[Product] for [specific person] who wants [outcome], sold as [angle], at [price]."*

**Stage 3: Scorecard.** Score it out of 100 using Section 4.

**Stage 4: Red team.** The Skeptic writes the three strongest reasons it fails. Each one gets answered or accepted as a risk.

**Stage 5: Verdict.**

| Verdict | Meaning | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| **RUN** | Score ≥ 75, red team answered, calendar works | Source, build creatives, launch test budget |
| **TEST** | Score 60–74 or one open risk | Cheap validation first (organic posts, a small ad test, a pre-order page) |
| **WATCH** | Promising but early, or wrong timing | Re-check on a set date, note the trigger that would upgrade it |
| **KILL** | Score < 60 or a fatal red-team point | Log why it died so we don't revisit it |

## 4. The scorecard (100 points)

| # | Criterion | Pts | What a top score looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Demand momentum** | 15 | Clear upward trend across 2+ independent sources in the last 30 days |
| 2 | **Wow / demonstrability** | 12 | The value is obvious in a 3-second video with no voiceover |
| 3 | **Margin** | 12 | ≥ 3x landed cost, perceived value well above price |
| 4 | **Q4 fit / giftability** | 12 | Works as a gift, rides a holiday moment, or solves a winter problem |
| 5 | **Angle depth** | 10 | 3+ distinct audiences or hooks, not just one |
| 6 | **Saturation headroom** | 10 | Few direct competitors, or a clearly untaken angle among many |
| 7 | **Fulfillment speed** | 8 | US/local stock or reliable ≤ 7-day delivery |
| 8 | **Problem / desire strength** | 8 | Painful problem or strong identity/status desire |
| 9 | **AOV and repeat potential** | 7 | Natural bundles, upsells, refills or collectibility |
| 10 | **Risk (inverse)** | 6 | No IP, battery, medical-claim, fragility or high-return issues |

**Hard kills, whatever the score:** trademarked or knockoff design, regulated health claims, can't arrive before the moment it's sold for, or landed-cost markup under 2x.

## 5. Signal sources

Ranked by how close they sit to real money.

**Tier A: money is moving.**
- TikTok Shop best-sellers and sales trackers (Kalodata, FastMoss, Shoplus)
- Amazon Movers & Shakers, Best Sellers, and "bought in past month" counts
- AliExpress or 1688 order velocity
- Etsy best-sellers and "in X carts" signals

**Tier B: someone is spending to sell it.**
- Meta Ad Library: how many ads, how many advertisers, how long the oldest has run (30+ days running usually means profitable)
- TikTok Creative Center: top ads and trending products
- Brands newly running ads at scale

**Tier C: attention without proof of buying.**
- TikTok and Reels views, comment sections asking "where do I get this?"
- Google Trends, Exploding Topics, Pinterest Trends
- Reddit threads, Discord, niche forums

**Rule:** a candidate needs at least one Tier A or Tier B signal before it can score above 60.

## 6. Creative lenses

The "smart and creative" part. Run every promising signal through at least three of these before scoring angle depth.

1. **Trend transplant.** Take a format that's working in one niche and move it to another. *(A viral kitchen gadget format, applied to car or pet accessories.)*
2. **Picks and shovels.** Don't sell the trend. Sell what people need *because* of the trend. *(Everyone's buying X, so what accessory, storage, refill or upgrade does X create demand for?)*
3. **Gift reframe.** Repackage a utility product as a gift for a specific person. "For the dad who has everything" beats "multi-tool."
4. **Persona swap.** Same product, new buyer. Pet owners, nurses, gamers, new moms, truckers, teachers, gym girls, RV people.
5. **Bundle or unbundle.** Turn a single item into a kit (higher AOV, harder to compare prices), or pull the hero piece out of a bloated kit.
6. **Price-tier jump.** Take a cheap viral item and make the premium version, or take a premium item and make the accessible version.
7. **Counter-trend.** When everyone zigs, what's the zag? *(Screen-time backlash, analog revival, "quiet" versions of loud products.)*
8. **Seasonal re-skin.** Can an evergreen winner get a Halloween, Christmas or winter version?
9. **Moment hijack.** Tie it to a cultural moment: a show, a game launch, a sports season, a meme.
10. **Problem reversal.** List the top complaints in a winning product's reviews. The fix is the next product.

## 7. Q4 2026 calendar `[MIRROR]`

Today is the start of October. Work backward from delivery dates, not from launch dates.

| Moment | Date | What it means for the desk |
|---|---|---|
| Halloween | Sat Oct 31 | Too late to source new Halloween product from overseas. Only US-stocked items. |
| Singles' Day (11.11) | Wed Nov 11 | Good for promos. Overseas suppliers get slow around it. |
| Thanksgiving | Thu Nov 26 | |
| **Black Friday** | **Fri Nov 27** | Peak CPMs. Have winners *proven* before this, not discovered during it. |
| Cyber Monday | Mon Nov 30 | |
| Overseas shipping cutoff (estimate) | ~Dec 1–5 | Last safe order date for 10–15 day China shipping before Christmas |
| US ground shipping cutoff (estimate) | ~Dec 15–18 | |
| Christmas | Fri Dec 25 | |
| Post-holiday / New Year | Dec 26 – Jan | CPMs drop hard. Gift-card spend, "new year, new me", organization, fitness |

**Timing rule:** a product discovered after **~Nov 10** must ship from domestic stock to be sold as a Christmas gift.

## 8. Unit economics quick math

```
Landed cost   = product + shipping to customer + packaging + payment fees
Break-even CPA = price − landed cost
Target         = price ≥ 3 × landed cost   (Q4 CPMs can run 30–60% above baseline)
```

If break-even CPA is under ~$20 at Q4 ad prices, the product needs a strong organic or creator angle to work.
