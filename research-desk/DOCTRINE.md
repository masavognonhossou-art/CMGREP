# Research Desk Doctrine

The operating rules for finding products to run in Q4 2026 and beyond.
Every session on this desk follows this document. When it and a gut feeling disagree, the doctrine wins until we change the doctrine on purpose.

> Status: **v0.2.** Rebuilt around organic dropshipping using Kane's product criteria (Section 4). Sections marked `[MIRROR]` still get rewritten once the reference screenshot is shared.

---

## 1. The ten rules

1. **Signal before story.** A product enters the pipeline because of evidence (high-intent comments, sellers actively posting and going viral, real order counts), not because it's cool.
2. **Demand is proven, never assumed.** If people aren't already paying for it, or for something close to it, it's a research project, not a product.
3. **Every claim gets a source and a date.** "It's blowing up" is not evidence. "3 sellers posted in the last 24h, top video 2.1M views, 40+ liked 'where do I buy' comments" is.
4. **Kill fast, kill cheap.** Most candidates die at the scorecard. That's the system working. A dead candidate costs ten minutes. A bad launch costs the month.
5. **Red-team everything.** Before any verdict the Skeptic argues the case *against*. If nobody can answer the Skeptic, it doesn't run.
6. **Margin is a filter, not an afterthought.** Organic means no ad spend, but refunds, slow shipping complaints and creator payouts still eat profit. Aim for 3x landed cost, never under 2x.
7. **The calendar is the boss.** In Q4 a great product that arrives late is a bad product. Fulfillment time decides what we can sell, and when.
8. **Angles beat products.** The same item can flop or print depending on who it's for and how it's framed. Every candidate needs at least three distinct angles before testing.
9. **Saturation is about the angle, not the product.** A product everyone sells can still work with a fresh angle, audience or offer. A product nobody sells may have no market.
10. **Write it down.** Every signal, score and verdict goes in [`LEDGER.md`](./LEDGER.md). The desk remembers so we don't re-research the same thing twice.

## 2. Desk roles

One person (you) plus Claude, wearing different hats. Each analysis names which hat is speaking.

| Role | Job | Asks |
|---|---|---|
| **Scout** | Finds raw signals | What's moving? Where did I see it? How fast? |
| **Analyst** | Turns signals into evidence | Is the demand real, growing, and buyable? |
| **Merchant** | Unit economics and logistics | Can we land it, ship it in time, and keep money after refunds and creator costs? |
| **Creative Director** | Angles, hooks, offers | Who's it for, what's the scroll-stopping moment, why now? |
| **Skeptic** | Red team | Why will this fail? Who already owns it? What's the hidden risk? |

## 3. The pipeline

Every candidate moves left to right. It can be killed at any stage.

```
SIGNAL  →  CANDIDATE  →  ORGANIC GATE  →  SCORED  →  RED-TEAMED  →  VERDICT
(raw)      (framed)      (Kane, §4)       (100-pt)   (Skeptic)      RUN / TEST / WATCH / KILL
```

**Stage 1: Signal intake.** Log what was seen, where, when, and the raw number (views, high-intent comments, seller's last post date, orders, price). Note whether it's a **burner find** (Track A) or a **find** (Track B). No judgment yet.

**Stage 2: Candidate framing.** Turn the signal into a sellable hypothesis in one line:
> *"[Product] for [specific person] who wants [outcome], sold as [angle], at [price]."*

**Stage 3: Organic gate.** Run the Track A or Track B checks in Section 4. Any clear fail kills it or sends it to WATCH.

**Stage 3b: Scorecard.** Score it out of 100 using Section 5.

**Stage 4: Red team.** The Skeptic writes the three strongest reasons it fails. Each one gets answered or accepted as a risk.

**Stage 5: Verdict.**

| Verdict | Meaning | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| **RUN** | Score ≥ 75, red team answered, calendar works | Order a sample, storyboard hooks, start posting |
| **TEST** | Score 60–74 or one open risk | Post 5–10 test videos on a fresh account before ordering stock |
| **WATCH** | Promising but early, or wrong timing | Re-check on a set date, note the trigger that would upgrade it |
| **KILL** | Score < 60 or a fatal red-team point | Log why it died so we don't revisit it |

## 4. The organic gate (Kane's criteria)

The desk runs **organic dropshipping**: products sell through viral TikTok/Reels/Shorts content, not paid ads. So the first question isn't "is the market growing?" It's **"is someone selling this with organic content right now, and can we go viral with it too?"**

Adapted from Kane's personal product criteria (organic dropshipper, $600K+). Every candidate passes this gate *before* it gets scored. It depends on where the product was found.

**No single criterion makes a winner. All of them have to line up.** A product with tons of "link?" comments can still be a dud (see Kane's water-chest example below).

### Track A: Burner finds
For products found by scrolling a burner account, where another dropshipper is already selling it.

| # | Check | Pass looks like | Fail looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **High-intent demand** | Comments saying "where do I buy this", "link?", "how do I get it", "I need this", with lots of likes on those comments | "I love this", "so cool", "need" with no buying intent |
| A2 | **Demand from buyers you can sell to** | Tap commenters' profiles: they're in the countries you ship to and look like your target buyer | Most "link?" comments come from markets you don't ship to or where the price is out of reach. That's fake demand for you |
| A3 | **Demand ratio** | At least ~10 high-intent comments per viral video, scaled to views | 2 "link?" comments on 1M views |
| A4 | **Actively posting** | Seller posted in the last 24 hours | Stopped posting. **Find out why** before killing it: moved platforms? Stopped going viral? Stopped converting? |
| A5 | **Actively going viral** | Recent videos (this week) still hitting. That's fresh proof the concept works now | Only old virals. Diagnose: bad content, worn-out concepts, or the audience has seen it too much. Can we break that with new pain points, audiences or concepts? |
| A6 | **Broad audience** | Lots of different kinds of people could want it. Room for a 5–6 figure run | Tight micro-niche that gets copied to death in two weeks for a quick $5–10K |
| A7 | **Saturation** | Search the product name *and* the viral video's on-screen text. Count competitors, and how many are still posting and going viral | Many sellers, most stopped, nobody going viral |
| A8 | **No primed-account trap** | Several sellers going viral, including fresh accounts | The only seller is a big, established account (20K+ followers) on one platform. Don't compete unless we have a genuinely different approach |

### Track B: Finds (AliExpress, Amazon, UGC creators)
For products not yet pushed by a dropshipper: a UGC creator showing a regular product, an AliExpress or Amazon listing.

| # | Check | Pass looks like |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | **Content strategy in hand** | We can describe the hook, the movements and the video structure *before* we commit. A winning product with no content plan is just another product |
| B2 | **Proof of concept from a similar product** | A similar item (same category, motion or use) has gone viral. Study its movements, hook and structure to borrow |
| B3 | **People actually buy it** | Real order counts on AliExpress or Amazon ("X bought in past month"), not just comment hype |
| B4 | **Common-sense check** | Would people actually take out a card and pay for this? Is a branded version already cheap at Walmart, Costco or Target? If yes, comments won't turn into sales |

**Kane's water-chest lesson:** a chest-mounted water drinker went hugely viral with "link link link" everywhere. It got 2 sales on millions of views. Two warning signs called it in advance: (1) a branded equivalent was already in big-box stores, and (2) a similar wrist version had already flopped. **When a similar product flopped, believe the flop over the comments.**

## 5. The scorecard (100 points, organic edition)

Only gate survivors get scored.

| # | Criterion | Pts | What a top score looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **High-intent demand** | 15 | Many liked "where do I buy" comments, from buyers in our markets, with a healthy ratio to views |
| 2 | **Live proof** | 12 | Multiple sellers posted in the last 24h and are going viral this week |
| 3 | **Wow / demonstrability** | 10 | Value is obvious in the first 3 seconds with no voiceover |
| 4 | **Broad audience** | 10 | Many kinds of buyers. Room for a big run, not a quick flip |
| 5 | **Saturation headroom** | 10 | Few active competitors or an untaken angle, and no primed-account trap |
| 6 | **Content strategy in hand** | 10 | We can storyboard 3+ distinct hooks and concepts today |
| 7 | **Margin** | 8 | ≥ 3x landed cost. No ad spend, but refunds, shipping and creator costs still bite |
| 8 | **Q4 fit / giftability** | 8 | Works as a gift or rides a holiday moment |
| 9 | **Fulfillment speed** | 7 | Arrives before the moment it's sold for |
| 10 | **Purchase reality** | 6 | Real order counts. Passes the common-sense test. No cheap branded version in big-box stores |
| 11 | **Risk (inverse)** | 4 | No IP, battery, medical-claim, fragility or high-return issues |

**Hard kills, whatever the score:**
- Trademarked or knockoff design, or regulated health claims
- Can't arrive before the moment it's sold for
- Landed-cost markup under 2x
- High-intent comments are mostly from markets we can't sell to
- A similar product already flopped and we have nothing that explains why ours would differ
- Track B find with no content strategy

**Soft kill (needs a written plan to survive):** the only competitor is a primed account on a single platform.

## 6. Signal sources

Ranked for organic dropshipping: how close the signal sits to proof that *organic content* is selling it.

**Tier A: organic content is selling it now.**
- Burner account: dropshipper videos with high-intent comments, seller posted in last 24h, going viral this week
- TikTok Shop best-sellers and sales trackers (Kalodata, FastMoss, Shoplus), which show sales tied to videos

**Tier B: people are buying it somewhere.**
- AliExpress or 1688 order counts; Amazon "bought in past month" and Movers & Shakers
- A similar product (same category or motion) that has gone viral and sold. This is Track B proof of concept
- Meta Ad Library: long-running ads mean someone profits from it, even if not organically

**Tier C: attention without proof of buying.**
- UGC creator videos with comment hype but no seller attached yet
- Google Trends, Exploding Topics, Pinterest Trends, market reports
- Reddit threads, Discord, niche forums

**Rule:** a candidate needs a Tier A signal, or a Tier B signal plus a written content strategy, before it can score above 60.

## 7. Creative lenses

The "smart and creative" part. Run every promising signal through at least three of these before scoring content strategy. These are also the tools for breaking view saturation (§4, A5).

1. **Trend transplant.** Take a format that's working in one niche and move it to another. *(A viral kitchen gadget format, applied to car or pet accessories.)*
2. **Picks and shovels.** Don't sell the trend. Sell what people need *because* of the trend. *(Everyone's buying X, so what accessory, storage, refill or upgrade does X create demand for?)*
3. **Gift reframe.** Repackage a utility product as a gift for a specific person. "For the dad who has everything" beats "multi-tool."
4. **Persona swap.** Same product, new buyer. Pet owners, nurses, gamers, new moms, truckers, teachers, gym girls, RV people.
5. **Bundle or unbundle.** Turn a single item into a kit (higher AOV, harder to compare prices), or pull the hero piece out of a bloated kit.
6. **Price-tier jump.** Take a cheap viral item and make the premium version, or take a premium item and make the accessible version.
7. **Counter-trend.** When everyone zigs, what's the zag? *(Screen-time backlash, analog revival, "quiet" versions of loud products.)*
8. **Seasonal re-skin.** Can an evergreen winner get a Halloween, Christmas or winter version?
9. **Moment hijack.** Tie it to a cultural moment: a show, a game launch, a sports season, a meme.
10. **Problem reversal.** List the top complaints in a winning product's reviews. The fix is the next product.

## 9. Unit economics quick math

```
Landed cost   = product + shipping to customer + packaging + payment fees
Profit/order  = price − landed cost − refund allowance (~5–10%) − creator payout (if any)
Target         = price ≥ 3 × landed cost
```

Organic has no ad cost per sale, so margin matters less than for paid ads. What matters more is **volume**: a broad audience (§4, A6) is what turns a viral video into a 5–6 figure run.
